🍁 Montreal at a Glance

Montreal is the largest city in Quebec and one of North America's most vibrant destinations. Blending French heritage, North American culture and artistic creativity, the city stands out for its unique identity and welcoming atmosphere.

From Old Montreal and Mount Royal to the Plateau-Mont-Royal, Mile End and Downtown, each neighbourhood has its own personality, combining historic streets, colourful murals, trendy cafés and beautiful green spaces.

With world-famous festivals, authentic Quebec cuisine, walks along the St. Lawrence River, fascinating museums, rooftop bars and a lively nightlife, Montreal offers an unforgettable experience in every season.

✈️ Before You Go to Montreal

🗣️ Language

French is the official language of Quebec and the main language spoken in Montreal. English is also widely used in hotels, restaurants, shops and major tourist attractions.

💵 Currency

The Canadian dollar (CAD) is used in Montreal. Credit and debit cards are accepted almost everywhere, although carrying a small amount of cash can be useful for markets and independent shops.

🚇 Getting Around

The STM metro and bus network makes it easy to reach Downtown, Old Montreal, the Plateau, Mile End and the city's main neighbourhoods. Walking and cycling are also enjoyable during the warmer months.

📅 Ideal Length of Stay

Four to five days are enough to explore the main neighbourhoods, museums, the Old Port and Mount Royal. A full week gives you more time to visit Quebec City, the Laurentians or the Eastern Townships.

🌤️ Best Time to Visit

From May to October, temperatures are generally pleasant and the city hosts many festivals. Winter offers a completely different atmosphere, with snow, ice skating and seasonal activities.

💰 Average Budget

Plan to spend around 140 to 320 Canadian dollars per person per day, depending on your accommodation, dining choices and booked activities.

⚠️ Mistakes to Avoid in Montreal

A few common mistakes can make a trip to Montreal less enjoyable, especially because of the city's size, changing weather and local customs.

🗺️ Underestimating the Distance Between Neighbourhoods

Montreal is easy to explore, but the Old Port, the Plateau, Mile End, Jean-Drapeau Park and Mount Royal are not all within walking distance of one another. Group your visits by area to avoid unnecessary travel.

🌡️ Not Checking the Weather Before Going Out

Temperatures can change quickly, especially in spring, autumn and winter. Wear suitable clothing and bring several light layers that are easy to remove.

💳 Forgetting About Taxes and Tips

Prices displayed in shops and restaurants usually do not include sales tax. In table-service restaurants, leaving a tip is also customary.

🚗 Using a Car for Every Journey

Traffic and parking can be difficult in Downtown and Old Montreal. The metro, buses, cycling and walking are often more convenient.

🎟️ Waiting Until the Last Minute to Book

Popular experiences such as AURA, Lachine Rapids excursions, food tours and major events can sell out during weekends and peak travel periods.

🏙️ Staying Only in Old Montreal

The historic district is essential, but Montreal is also best experienced through the Plateau, Mile End, Saint-Laurent Boulevard, Jean-Talon Market and its many residential neighbourhoods.

❓ Montreal FAQ

📅 How Many Days Do You Need in Montreal?

Four days are enough to discover the city's main neighbourhoods and attractions. Five to seven days are better for exploring at a more relaxed pace and adding a day trip beyond Montreal.

🏨 Which Neighbourhood Is Best for a First Visit to Montreal?

Downtown and Old Montreal are especially convenient for a first stay. Plateau-Mont-Royal and Mile End are better suited to travellers looking for a more local, creative and lively atmosphere.

🗣️ Can You Visit Montreal Without Speaking French?

Yes. English is widely understood in tourist areas, hotels, restaurants and shops. However, using a few words of French is always appreciated.

🚇 Can You Visit Montreal Without a Car?

Yes. The metro and bus network serves the city's main neighbourhoods efficiently. Downtown, Old Montreal, the Plateau and Mile End can also be explored on foot or by bike.

🍽️ What Food Should You Try in Montreal?

Poutine, Montreal-style bagels, smoked meat, maple syrup specialties and the city's many international cuisines are among the essential culinary experiences.

❄️ Is Montreal Worth Visiting in Winter?

Yes. The city has a unique atmosphere in winter, with snow, ice rinks, festive lights and seasonal activities. Very warm clothing and suitable footwear are essential.
