🇰🇷 Seoul at a Glance

Seoul is a fascinating capital where royal palaces stand alongside towering skyscrapers, traditional markets, creative cafés and one of Asia's most vibrant cultural scenes. Modern while proudly preserving its heritage, the city offers a unique experience with every visit.

From Myeong-dong to Hongdae, Gangnam to Seongsu-dong, and the charming Bukchon Hanok Village, every neighborhood has its own character. Shopping, authentic Korean cuisine, K-pop, rooftop bars, ancient temples and walks along the Han River create unforgettable experiences from morning until late at night.

Whether you're visiting for Korean food, Joseon Dynasty palaces, spectacular city views, unique cafés or the excitement of the Korean Wave, Seoul is a captivating destination where tradition blends seamlessly with innovation, creativity and a remarkable way of life.

⚠️ Mistakes to Avoid in Seoul

🚇 Underestimating the Distances

Seoul is a huge metropolis. Even with an excellent subway system, journeys can take time. Group your visits by neighborhood to make the most of each day.

🏯 Only Visiting Modern Neighborhoods

Gangnam and Hongdae are must-see districts, but the royal palaces, Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong reveal the city's remarkable historical heritage.

🎟️ Waiting Until the Last Minute

Some activities, guided tours and observation decks can sell out quickly, especially during holidays and cherry blossom season.

💳 Forgetting Your T-money Card

This rechargeable card makes traveling by subway, bus and certain taxis much easier. It also saves valuable time when making connections.

🤝 Ignoring Local Etiquette

Speaking quietly on public transport, respecting queues and following a few local etiquette rules will make your interactions with residents more pleasant.

✈️ Before You Travel to Seoul

🗣️ Language

Korean is the official language. English is commonly used at major tourist attractions, but learning a few Korean words is always appreciated.

💴 Currency

The local currency is the South Korean won (₩). Bank cards are widely accepted, but carrying some cash remains useful at certain markets.

🔌 Power Sockets

South Korea uses type C and F power sockets with a voltage of 220 V, the same as those used in France. An adapter is generally not required.

📅 Ideal Length of Stay

Four to five days are enough to discover Seoul's main neighborhoods. A week is ideal if you would also like to take one or two day trips.

🌸 Best Time to Visit

Spring offers beautiful cherry blossoms, while autumn brings spectacular colors and some of the best conditions for exploring Seoul.

💰 Average Budget

You should generally budget between €100 and €220 per day, depending on your accommodation, restaurants, activities and choice of transportation.

❓ Seoul FAQ

How Many Days Do You Need to Visit Seoul?

Four to five days are enough to explore the main neighborhoods, royal palaces, markets, panoramic viewpoints and must-have experiences. A week is ideal if you would also like to take a day trip outside the capital.

What Is the Best Neighborhood for a First Visit?

Myeong-dong is often the best choice thanks to its central location, excellent subway connections and wide selection of hotels, restaurants and shops.

Can You Visit Seoul Without Speaking Korean?

Yes. Major subway stations, attractions and many businesses provide information in English. Translation apps also make communication much easier.

Is Korean Food Very Spicy?

Some Korean specialties are spicy, but many dishes are mildly seasoned or not spicy at all. Restaurants generally offer a wide selection to suit every taste.

What Is the Best Season to Visit Seoul?

Spring and autumn are the most pleasant seasons, with mild temperatures, colorful landscapes and numerous festivals and events.

What Is the Easiest Way to Get Around Seoul?

Seoul's subway system is one of the most efficient in the world. Combined with a T-money card, it provides easy access to all of the capital's main neighborhoods.
